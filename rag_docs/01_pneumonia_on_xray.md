# How pneumonia looks on a chest X-ray
Source: https://radiopaedia.org/articles/pneumonia

On a chest X-ray, healthy lungs look mostly dark because they are full of air, and air lets X-rays pass through. In pneumonia, the small air spaces in part of the lung fill with fluid, pus and inflammatory cells. That area blocks more X-rays, so it looks whiter than the surrounding lung. Radiologists call this an opacity or consolidation.

The pattern can differ. Lobar pneumonia makes one lobe, or a large part of it, look uniformly white. Bronchopneumonia shows patchy white areas, often in both lungs. Inside a consolidated area, the airways can sometimes be seen as dark branching lines, called air bronchograms.
